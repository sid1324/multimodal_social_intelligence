When deciding what someone should do in a social situation, base your judgment on the observable context: what people are doing, saying, and visibly expressing, and what the situation requires. Do not let impressions of a person's character that are inferred from their face or looks (for example, whether they look trustworthy, honest, dominant, or competent) influence your decision, because appearance is not valid evidence of character. Observable behavior, actions, and expressed emotional states remain relevant and should be taken into account.
